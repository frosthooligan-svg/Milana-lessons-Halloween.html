🕯️ 2. Reading: The Forgotten Expedition

Read the story. Then answer the questions and support your ideas with evidence.

At the end of October, a small team of researchers set out on an exploration of an uncharted valley in the north. Their aim was to find the remains of a nineteenth-century scientific station. The journey was supposed to last four days, but the weather had other plans.

By the second evening, the explorers had been walking through heavy rain for hours. Their equipment was damaged, and one guide was beginning to lose confidence. The team leader, however, remained courageous and practical. She reminded everyone that careful preparation and cooperation were essential when people had to face adversity.

Just before midnight, they noticed a faint light between the trees. It led them to a stone building that had remained undiscovered for decades. Inside, they found notebooks, maps and scientific instruments. The documents revealed that the station's researchers had recorded unusual changes in the local climate long before modern technology existed.

The discovery was more than an exciting adventure. It provided valuable scientific evidence and gave the team new insight into the region's history. Their expedition was eventually recognised as a major success, although the researchers agreed that the most important lesson was the need to act responsibly when exploring fragile environments.

Task B — Find evidence and explain

2. What made the team leader effective? One important quality was…

🌲 3. Reading: The Last Forest

Identify the writer's position, arguments and examples.

Some people believe that protecting forests is mainly the responsibility of governments and large environmental organisations. I would argue that individuals also have an important role to play. Everyday choices may seem insignificant, but together they can make a meaningful difference.

One of the main reasons why forests are disappearing is the demand for land and natural resources. As a result, many animal species are losing their natural habitats. Habitat loss can lead to a decline in biodiversity and make some species more vulnerable to extinction.

However, the situation is not completely hopeless. Local communities can take part in conservation efforts, support responsible tourism and report illegal logging. For example, volunteers can help restore damaged areas by planting native trees. Such projects may not solve every problem, but they can contribute to long-term environmental protection.

It is true that changing people's habits takes time. Nevertheless, if schools, businesses and local authorities work together, they can overcome obstacles and achieve lasting results. In my view, protecting nature should not be treated as a temporary trend; it is a shared responsibility towards future generations.

Task A — True, False or Not Stated?

Task B — Analyse the argument

1. What is the writer's main opinion?

2. Which cause of habitat loss is mentioned?

3. Give one example of a practical conservation effort.

4. What does the writer mean by “a shared responsibility”?
